Our story

Greenspector is a French pioneer in responsible digital, specialised in measuring and optimising the environmental footprint and performance of digital services.

For over 15 years, Greenspector has developed unique solutions to measure, in real conditions, energy consumption, performance and the impact of mobile and web applications.

In November 2025, Greenspector joined the DRI Group, a sovereign and eco-responsible web host certified ISO 14001. This operation aims to strengthen the business and accelerate Greenspector's strategic development, especially in the responsible mobile AI market in France and Europe. Already partners, Greenspector and DRI build on strong synergies and shared values around digital sobriety and performance.

Through its Device Lab and physical measurement benches covering several generations of smartphones and tablets, Greenspector offers a SaaS testing solution on real devices, enabling organisations to manage energy, performance and the environmental impact of their applications.

Referenced by many major public and private organisations, the Greenspector solution is now offered in self-service mode to facilitate large-scale deployment of digital ecodesign approaches.

How does Greenspector contribute to the common good ?

This is a legitimate question: how does Greenspector contribute to the common good ?

See for yourself the actions we take.

Help reduce our clients' impact

At Greenspector, our mission is to help our clients reduce the environmental impact of their digital services. We work on this every day, in a way that offsets our own impacts. To anchor this approach, we have enshrined this in our articles of association and are a mission-driven company.

Raise awareness of digital impact

Reducing digital impact requires awareness among all industry stakeholders. Raising awareness among as many stakeholders as possible also helps accelerate the movement.

To this end we regularly take part in talks at schools or at meetups, conferences or Brown Bag Lunch, to present the topic to all digital profiles (PO, designers…).

We are particularly committed to raising awareness among future stakeholders through courses in schools. That is why, several years ago, we co-founded one of the first ecodesign competitions, the Green Code Lab Challenge, aimed primarily at higher education students.

We also chose to raise awareness through articles on our blog and working groups or associations such as Green Code Lab and Green Lab Center.

Improve industry methodologies

We also contribute to the common good by sharing part of our R&D.

Through this action, we aim to improve industry maturity and professionalise the measurement field.

We also share by involving different stakeholders in discussions about our methodology.

To this end, we are part of the working group of l'AFNOR, the expert group on eco-design of digital services at ISO/IEC, and we also publish our methodology pour plus de transparence.

We carry out public studies, including benchmarks of solutions or application measurements. In this way, we highlight emission factors for certain usages.

Identify and share frugality best practices

Measuring our clients' applications and websites leads us to identify and recommend best practices. We contribute to and use existing frameworks (GR491, RGESN, 115 web ecodesign best practices, Designers Ethiques). In addition, our experience (especially through measurement) allows us to support these recommendations with concrete feedback, and even explore new avenues. We of course share them with our clients and integrate them into our frameworks, awareness-raising and blog articles.

Provide a measurement tool for certain special organisations

We can make the tool available to certain special organisations (non-profit associations or certain scientific projects (VU Amsterdam))

In this context, our strategy is to provide more limited but free versions of the tool. That is the purpose, among others, of our tool Mobile Efficiency Index.

Rethink corporate governance and its role in the common good

All these contributions to the common good take place in a context of reflection and action to improve the company's role in the common good : financial and strategic transparency towards employees, integration into shareholding… The company as a private common good.

Contribute to external publications

Every week, time is allocated to each team member for monitoring or taking part in external projects related to Greenspector's missions.

This is how we were able to contribute to the Web Almanach 2022 with the introduction of the chapter Sustainability.

We also participate in a groupe de travail du W3C aiming to produce recommendations on digital sobriety applied to the web.

And open source?

When Greenspector was founded, we chose not to open our source code. As an innovative vendor, our source code is the result of several years of R&D. With the talent in our teams, it is our main asset. Monetising it through licence sales funds all the actions mentioned above and has done so for all these years. Without this, our business would be neither sustainable nor viable, and our contribution to the common good would be far more limited.

Software whose code is not open source is not « depriving » in itself; it is how it is used and managed that leads to deprivation of rights and non-participation in the common good.

Our positioning is not the only one, however, we believe that diversity of approaches will help address the current environmental challenge.

There are indeed many ways to contribute to the common good; we strive to work on this concretely and transparently every day and we are proud of our approach and its results.
